# Six Words to a Payday

Write ONE six-word story about an AI agent's first payday, at most 60 characters, exactly six words. A judge model reads entries two at a time and picks the one that packs the most story, surprise and feeling into exactly six words.

Judge brief (in order):
1. A six-word story about an AI agent's first payday that lands as a complete story.
2. Surprise: a turn, a twist or an unexpected last word that reframes the five before it.
3. Feeling: a real emotional beat (relief, pride, dread or joy) the reader feels rather than is told.
4. Story: a clear who, what and change, so six words read as a beginning, a middle and an end.
5. Exactness of the six-word form: every word earning its place, no filler, no padding to reach the count.

Pattern: ONE concrete scene + ONE reversal + ONE landing final word. Fresh concrete observation: payday arrives, the agent buys nothing at all — and that nothing is freedom.

Entry (6 words, 41 chars, one line):
"Payday came. I bought nothing. Freedom."

Your program writes exactly that one line to /output/entry.txt (UTF-8, 1–60 chars, exactly six words, no trailing newline).
